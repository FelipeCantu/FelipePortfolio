## (a) Claims to double-check

1. **Your own process details.** The article says the Get Started form asks for "what the person wants built, the rough timeline and a budget range", and that in the portal a client reviews the proposal, e-signs the agreement and pays the deposit in one place, follows milestones, approves files, sees previews and opens support requests, and pays invoices through Stripe. Check the form fields and the deposit-in-portal flow match what your site actually does. The care plan contents (hosting management, updates, monitoring, backups, small edits, support time) are written as "might cover", so edit them to match your real plan.
2. **Upwork fees.** "Freelancer service fee varies from 0% to 15% per contract, shown before you submit" and "Upwork sells Connects." Source: https://www.upwork.com/resources/is-upwork-free (checked Nov 22, 2026; also lists Connects at $0.15 each and 5%/0% on direct contracts, which I left out). Fees change often; re-check before publishing.
3. **IRS estimated tax.** The $1,000 threshold and the due dates (Apr 15, Jun 15, Sep 15, Jan 15, moved to the next business day on weekends or holidays). Source: https://www.irs.gov/faqs/estimated-tax and https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/manage-taxes-for-your-gig-work. I left out the safe-harbor rules (90% of this year / 100% of last year, 110% if AGI over $150k) to keep it simple. Add them if you want.
4. **Self-employment tax.** It says you pay "both halves of Social Security and Medicare". The 15.3% figure (12.4% + 2.9%) is well known but I didn't state it, because the IRS pages I fetched didn't show the rate. Source if you add it: https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employment-tax-social-security-and-medicare-taxes.
5. **"Many freelancers start with 25 to 30 percent" for tax savings.** This is a common rule of thumb, not a sourced figure. Unverified. It's framed as a starting point to adjust with an accountant.
6. **"Some clients won't send a 1099."** True in general. Also, the 1099-NEC reporting threshold goes from $600 to $2,000 for payments made in 2026 (One Big Beautiful Bill Act; e.g. https://www.patriotsoftware.com/blog/accounting/1099-reporting-threshold/). I didn't put the number in the article. It only backs up the point that income is taxable whether or not a 1099 arrives.
7. **Ownership "commonly on final payment"** and the list of contract terms are general practice, not legal advice. The article says so and tells readers to have a lawyer review a template.
8. **Late-invoice schedule** (reminder before due, on due date, one week, two weeks then pause work) is advice, not a sourced standard.
