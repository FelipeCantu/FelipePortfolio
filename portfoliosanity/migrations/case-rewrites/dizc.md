Dizc is a desktop music player built for people who take their local music library seriously. Streaming platforms dominate listening now, and Dizc brings the focus back to owning your music: your files, your collection, your way. The goal was to build something that feels as premium as the music it plays.

## The problem

Most local music players haven't evolved in years. They are either bloated with features nobody uses, stuck with outdated interfaces, or missing the small details that make an app feel polished. Dizc was built to fix that with a clean, focused player that gets out of the way and just sounds good.

## Features

- Playback for common audio formats.
- A dark interface designed for low-light listening sessions.
- Library organization by artist, album and genre.
- Smooth seek and volume controls.
- Album artwork shown in a clean, full layout.
- A minimal design that keeps the music front and center.

## Design decisions

Every design decision in Dizc was intentional. The dark palette reduces eye strain during long listening sessions. Type was chosen to read at a glance. Controls sit where your hands naturally reach, and nothing is hidden behind menus that don't need to be there.

## What I learned

Building Dizc pushed me to think about user experience at the smallest scale: hover states, transition timing, spacing that feels right. It reinforced that great UI isn't about adding more. It's about refining until nothing is left to remove.
